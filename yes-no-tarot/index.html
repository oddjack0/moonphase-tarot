How It Works

Yes-or-no tarot is the simplest reading in the deck, and that simplicity is the point. One card, one answer, no elaborate spread to decode — just a direct response you can sit with and think about.

The rule is straightforward. Upright cards lean toward yes and reversed cards lean toward no, because an upright card shows its energy flowing freely while a reversed card shows it blocked, delayed or turned inward. But five cards refuse to be forced into a binary: the Hanged Man, the Wheel of Fortune, Temperance, the Two of Swords and the Four of Swords always resolve to maybe, whatever way up they land.

That maybe is not a cop-out. It is the reading being honest: some questions genuinely have no clean answer yet, or the timing is wrong, or the question itself needs rethinking. A maybe is an invitation to pause, gather more information, or ask a sharper question — not a reason to keep drawing until a yes appears.

Above all, this is a tool for reflection, not fortune-telling. A yes can give you the courage to act on what you already wanted; a no can surface a doubt worth listening to; a maybe can stop a rushed decision. The value is in what the answer makes you notice about your own mind, and every reading here is for entertainment and self-reflection.

Good Questions

  • “Should I accept this job offer?” — one decision, one moment.
  • “Is now a good time to launch my project?” — clear timing, clear scope.
  • “Should I reach out to them first?” — a specific action you control.
  • “Would moving to the new flat be right for me?” — a single choice, honestly asked.
Avoid Questions Like

Questions That Won’t Work

  • “What will happen in my life?” — too broad for a single card.
  • “When exactly will I meet someone?” — tarot does not give dates or guarantees.
  • “Should I do A, B or C?” — three options need a spread, not a yes/no draw.
  • “What does someone else secretly think?” — other people's minds are not yours to read.

Yes/No Questions

How does the yes/no tarot reading work?

You focus on a question, draw one card, and the reading resolves to yes, no, or maybe. Upright cards lean toward yes and reversed cards lean toward no, while cards that are inherently ambiguous always resolve to maybe, whatever way up they land.

Which cards always give a maybe?

Five cards are inherently ambiguous and always read as maybe: the Hanged Man, the Wheel of Fortune, Temperance, the Two of Swords and the Four of Swords. A yes or no answer from these cards would be misleading, so the reading says so honestly.

Should I ask the same question more than once?

It is better to ask once and sit with the answer. Drawing repeatedly until you get the answer you want turns a reflection tool into a slot machine. If the answer was maybe, rephrase the question more precisely and draw again.

Are yes/no tarot readings accurate?

Tarot readings are for entertainment and self-reflection, not prediction. Treat a yes, no or maybe as a prompt for honest thinking about your question, not as a guarantee about what will happen.
